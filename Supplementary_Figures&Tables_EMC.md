2.  **Supplementary Methods**

*1.1 Sequential Filtration*

We assembled two filter towers each consisting of a 1-L side-arm flask
with a 47-mm filter funnel (Pall Corp). Using vacuum pressure, we used
the first tower to filter 200 mL of each sample through a 1.2-μm
polycarbonate membrane filter (Sterilitech, Auburn, WA), collecting the
flowthrough in the flask. We then filtered this flowthrough with the
second tower, this time using a 0.4-μm polycarbonate membrane filter
(Sterilitech, Auburn, WA). Using flame-sterilized forceps, we folded
each filter and transferred it into a 2-mL polypropylene microcentrifuge
tube (VWR, Radnor, PA) with 700 μL of cetyltrimethylammonium (CTAB)
buffer. We rinsed each filtration funnel and flask thoroughly with
deionized water before filtration of the subsequent sample, and we
autoclaved all filtration equipment between time points. Finally, we
stored all filters at -20 °C prior to eDNA extraction.

*1.2 Extraction Protocol*

First, we added 20 μL of proteinase K to each microcentrifuge tube
containing an eDNA sample and incubated for 2 hr at 59°C to digest
proteins. Next, we gently mixed the digested filters with 900 μL of
25:24:1 phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol and centrifuged at 15,000 g
for 15 min at room temperature to separate the aqueous phase containing
DNA from the organic phase. After the initial centrifugation, we
pipetted the aqueous supernatant (~700 µL) into a new microcentrifuge
tube, added 700 µL of chloroform:isoamyl alcohol (24:1), inverted to
mix, and centrifuged it again at 15,000 g for 12 min to ensure all
phenol was removed. We pipetted the resulting supernatant (~650 µL) into
a new tube, added 600 μL of isopropanol and 300 μL of 5M NaCl, gently
mixed, and precipitated overnight (≥8 hrs) at -20 °C. We then
centrifuged the solution at 15,000x g for 15 min and washed the
resulting DNA pellet twice with 200 μL of ice-cold 70% ethanol,
centrifuging for 5 min between each wash. We dried the pellets at room
temperature until all ethanol evaporated, resuspending in 50 μL of warm
(60 °C) TE buffer before heating to 39 °C for 10 min, and holding the
samples overnight at 4 °C. After extraction, eDNA samples were stored at
-20 °C prior to quantification.

*1.3 ddPCR Quantification Protocol*

We ran both assays in duplex with n=3 technical replicates for each
sample. Each reaction consisted of 10 μL of ddPCR Supermix for Probes
(BioRad, Hercules, CA), 0.18 μL of each primer (900 nM reaction
concentration), 0.05 μL of each probe (250 nM reaction concentration), 4
μL of extracted target DNA, and 5 μL of molecular water, for a total
volume of 20 μL. Finally, we used the following thermocycling
conditions: 95 °C /10 min, 40 x (94 °C / 30 s, 57 °C / 1 min), 98 °C 10
min.

Following amplification and droplet fluorescence reading, we isolated
positive and negative droplets with a manual, user-defined threshold in
QuantaSoft (BioRad, Hercules, CA), and calculated the concentration of
target DNA using the positive and negative droplet counts from three
technical replicates to produce one measurement per sample. From this
value, we calculated the concentration of target eDNA in the sampled
water.

Our experimental design included negative controls across multiple
stages of the study, including a filtration control during each time
point, an extraction control with each set of extractions, and four
negative PCR reactions with each 96-well plate of ddPCR. Out of the 81
negative control wells, five contained a single positive droplet, below
the limit of detection (3.2 / 3.6 gene copies per well), indicating that
contamination between wells was both infrequent and insignificant.

*1.4 Microbial Respiration: Measurement and Calculation.*

Briefly, prior to the experiment, we collected two 50-mL portions of
water from each mesocosm in 50-mL centrifuge tubes, one of which was
opaque. We then recorded the starting dissolved oxygen (DO)
concentration (mg O<sub>2</sub> L<sup>-1</sup>), % saturation, and
temperature using a handheld DO probe before placing the centrifuge
tubes back into their respective mesocosms and securing them to the
bottom. We allowed the tubes to incubate for three hours and then we
removed the tubes and measured the final DO concentration and
temperature, as well as the exact length of the incubation period for
each tube. For the mesocosms that contained leaves, we also performed
the same light/dark incubations using 50 mL of mesocosm water but with
the inclusion of five small (diameter = 1.85 cm) disks cut from the
leaves in each mesocosm to assess the metabolic contribution of leaf
biofilms. As before, we measured DO concentration and temperature before
and after the three-hour incubation. We calculated microbial respiration
rates for both the water-alone and leaf + water incubations as the
difference of dissolved oxygen within each centrifuge tube divided by
water volume and leaf area. We expressed the leaf respiration rate as
the difference in respiration between the leaf incubations and the water
incubations. **
